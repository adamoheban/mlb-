Lockout Watch

Where owners and players stand, what has been offered, the deadlines, and what the reporting says could happen. Updated Nov 27.

No deal, and a lockout is widely expected on Dec. 1

The collective bargaining agreement expires at 11:59 p.m. ET on Dec. 1. The owners want a salary cap and floor. The players' union says it will never accept one. Talks continue, but the sides remain far apart. MLB Trade Rumors ↗

Where each side stands

Owners / MLB

  • Proposes a hard salary cap and a payroll floor, tied to a 50-50 split of revenue with players. The first offer set a 2027 cap of $245.3 million and a floor of $171.2 million. MLB.com ↗
  • On Nov. 3 the league delayed the cap and floor to 2028 and raised the numbers to a $258.2 million cap and a $180.2 million floor. MLB.com ↗
  • Under the Nov. 3 plan, teams could fall to 90% of the floor if they spend the full floor over three years, and individual contracts would also be capped. MLB says it would split revenue 50-50. AP via ADN ↗
  • Wants all local media revenue pooled and shared equally among the 30 teams. Manfred has said all 30 clubs back that idea. ESPN ↗
  • Says all current contracts would stay guaranteed and that guaranteed contracts would not be banned under a cap. AP via ClickOnDetroit ↗
  • Argues a cap levels the playing field so more teams can compete. Needs 23 of 30 owners to ratify a deal, and eight could block one. Awful Announcing ↗
  • Commissioner Rob Manfred has said he worries the cap push could lead to a stoppage like 1994–95. AP via WRAL ↗

Players / MLBPA

  • Will not accept a salary cap in any form. Interim executive director Bruce Meyer has called a cap institutionalized collusion. MLB Trade Rumors ↗
  • Says a cap would erode guaranteed contracts because of an escrow clawback if the players' share rises above 50%. AP via ADN ↗
  • Says that using MLB's own definition of revenue, the players' share would go down. The union estimated a loss of about half a billion dollars had the plan applied in 2026. ESPN, via Bleacher Nation ↗
  • Opened with a plan that keeps the luxury tax: raise the threshold from $244 million to $300 million and add a penalty for teams that spend too little. AP via ABC7 ↗
  • Wants more local media revenue shared (close to 90%) and a guaranteed $240 million in revenue for every small-market club. ESPN ↗
  • Called the Nov. 3 delay irrelevant to its objections. The union wants the cap removed entirely and says the package still cuts pay. Boston Globe ↗
  • Says MLB has refused to negotiate economics beyond a cap since May, while the sides have made progress on non-economic items such as drug policy and grievance procedures. Union leader Bruce Meyer says players already receive about 55-56% of revenue. Boston Globe ↗
  • Says it stands ready to negotiate in good faith. Meyer has said both sides share an interest in not missing games. Front Office Sports ↗

Latest news

  • Nov 20
    Union rejects MLB's international draft; league drops a draft-eligibility change

    AP reports the union turned down the international draft and asked for larger bonus pools under the current system. MLB's latest offer included $200M in international bonus pools for 2027, hard slots and a minimum signing age of 18, and it dropped its plan to bar high school players from the domestic draft. AP says a lockout is expected next month. AP via Seattle Sports ↗

  • Nov 18
    Manfred says owners will not drop the cap; lockout decision "at the last possible moment"

    At a news conference, Manfred said owners will not give up the cap demand and that the lockout call would come late; the league will bargain whenever needed. MLB exec Morgan Sword said 2026 league revenue was $12.9B. Union head Meyer said the stance shows the league is not serious and accused owners of seeking lower pay; the league said the union has made no major economic proposal since its opener. AP via ADN ↗

  • Nov 17
    Union head says MLB is not serious about avoiding a lockout

    Meyer said the league has ignored union proposals that lack a cap. An MLB spokesperson said the league has offered earlier free agency and higher pay for younger players, and argued the union plan would widen payroll gaps. No new proposals were reported; the union board meets in Scottsdale at the end of November. Boston Globe ↗

  • Nov 12
    GM meetings wrap up; owners meet next week

    General managers met Nov. 10–12. Owners meet Nov. 17–19, and the players' annual meeting follows before the Dec. 1 deadline. SB Nation (Southside Sox) ↗

    MLB delays the cap to 2028 and raises the numbers; union rejects it

    The league proposed a $258.2M cap and $180.2M floor starting in 2028, a $1M minimum salary and a bigger bonus pool. The union rejected the package within hours. MLB Trade Rumors ↗

What could happen

A lockout starts Dec. 1

This is the widely reported expectation. AP reports MLB is likely to lock out the players as soon as the deal expires, and the union has told players to expect a stoppage. One betting market priced a new deal before the deadline at about 5% (the page is undated, so the number may be stale). Covers ↗

Deal reached by March, season starts on time or a bit late

The last lockout lasted 99 days, ended March 10, 2022, and pushed Opening Day back about a week while keeping 162 games. Executives and agents surveyed by FanSided gave predictions ranging from an on-time start to a 2–4 week delay. One betting market gave about a 67% chance the season starts before April 30 (undated, may be stale). FanSided / Covers ↗

Longer stoppage, games lost

Some insiders expect a late start. USA Today's Bob Nightengale reported executives expecting a start around Memorial Day, and one anonymous pitcher told ESPN he expects a lockout lasting until June. Both reports are opinions, not forecasts of the outcome. ClutchPoints ↗

The 2027 season is lost

This is the least likely scenario in the reporting, but not impossible. The NHL lost its entire 2004–05 season to a lockout, and the 1994–95 baseball strike canceled the World Series. One executive in FanSided's survey predicted no baseball. FanSided ↗

These scenarios summarize published reporting and insider opinion. They are not predictions by this site, and betting-market figures may be out of date.

Past stoppages

  • 2021–22 lockout: 99 days. The deal came March 10, 2022, and Opening Day moved from March 31 to April 7. Pitcher List ↗
  • 1994–95 strike: A 7½-month strike over an owners' cap proposal canceled the World Series for the first time in 90 years. AP via WRAL ↗
